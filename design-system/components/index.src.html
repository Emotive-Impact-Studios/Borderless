The work is the window. Everything else floats.

Borderless uses Apple’s Glass language. Content carries no chrome. Who put it there, and whether it is live, floats beneath it in a glass tag. Each person’s colour glows when their work arrives. One system covers four looks, so the same room suits a boardroom, a classroom, a studio or a control room.

Principles

Four rules every screen follows

The work is the hero

Content fills the screen. Chrome lives in glass that floats outside the content and fades when nobody needs it.

Motion tells the truth

Travel starts when bytes leave. A window lands only when the hub confirms it. Nothing celebrates success that has not happened.

Calm at rest, alive on arrival

The resting Wall is quiet. The energy goes into the second when something crosses over, in the sender’s colour.

Readable from the back row

Nothing on the Wall is smaller than 18 px at 1080p. Owner colour never works alone: every item also shows an initial and a name.

Colour

Semantic colour, swapped by look

Components only use these roles, so changing the look never changes the structure. Every pair passes the contrast check in the build.

Owner colours

Typography

SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter everywhere else

Two ramps. The Portal follows Apple’s text styles. The Wall is sized for a 55-inch 1080p screen seen from 3 metres and doubles at 4K. Executive swaps in Playfair Display for headings, and Tactical swaps in IBM Plex Mono.

Space, shape and depth

A 4 px grid with concentric corners

Inner radius equals outer radius minus padding, so nested shapes stay parallel, as they do in Apple’s hardware and software.
